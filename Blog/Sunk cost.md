## The Mental Conflict

In data, the most dangerous number is Zero. Not because it represents nothing, but because it’s the cost of doing nothing. What we call 'Safety' is, in reality, can be 'Systemic Decay’. Today, we are talking about why your brain is programmed to let you lose.

As a matter of fact, your brain is fighting a silent war: **Status Quo Bias** vs. **Opportunity Cost Awareness**.

- **The Push:** One side craves safety and predictability to conserve neural energy. The other side hungers for growth and optimization.
- **The Pull:** We are caught in a tug-of-war between **Loss Aversion** and **Regret Aversion**.

In *Thinking, Fast and Slow*, Daniel Kahneman demonstrates that the pain of losing is twice as powerful as the joy of gaining. Scientifically, for your brain to greenlight a risky move, the potential reward must be **1.5 to 2.5 times** greater than the potential penalty. Only then does the brain signal: 'Okay, this wager is worth the entry.'

## Statement: The Misunderstanding Mindset

In finance, the most basic formula is: $Revenue - Expense = Profit$.

But the real problem lies in the word 'Expense.' Usually, we only see **Explicit Costs**—the visible numbers on a Balance Sheet. But when we apply this to our personal lives, we encounter **Implicit Costs** and **Psychological Costs**.
**Sunk Cost** is the rusted anchor holding your ship steady in the middle of a storm. You’re so worried about losing the chain that you don't dare cut it to sail toward safety. When the opportunity costs of staying still versus moving forward seem equal, Sunk Cost is what pins us down. We grieve the effort and time already spent, so we continue pouring resources into a 'sunk hole,' even when the future returns no longer justify the investment.

## Science Proof: Why our brain lies?

Why is it so hard to let go? According to Prospect Theory, our brains categorized 'giving up the old' as a certain Loss, while the new path is merely a potential Gain.

Combined with the Endowment Effect, we overvalue what we already own simply because it’s ours. Your Comfort Zone feels like 'Autopilot,' while making a new decision requires the Prefrontal Cortex to perform the cognitive equivalent of a high-intensity workout. Your brain is naturally frugal with energy, so it whispers: 'Don’t change. Just leave it be.

## Laboratory Challenge: The 500 Million Test

et’s run a psychological financial experiment. Imagine you have 500 million VND in savings after years of hard work:

- **Option 1 (Status Quo):** You keep it in a savings account at 6% interest. Everything is on **Autopilot**. No new learning, no stress. You sleep soundly because the balance grows predictably. This is a very seductive 'Green Zone.'

- **Option 2 (The Sunk Cost creator):** You enter the Index Fund market, projecting a 10% CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate). But this isn't a straight line—it’s a high-volatility environment. But look at the price you pay *before* the profit arrives:
    - You spend 20 hours of your weekend dissecting prospectuses.
    - You struggle for three days with eKYC and brokerage setups—tasks that feel incredibly daunting as The Uninformed Professional.
    - You stay up until 2:00 AM researching NAV (Net Asset Value), market volatility, and crowd psychology.
    - **Most importantly:** You’ve conquered the fear of being scammed and the terror of losing money to place that first order.

Now, the question isn't just 6% vs. 10%. Suppose after three months, the market shakes and your fund drops 2%. If you quit now to go back to savings, you aren’t just losing a few million in cash. What you’re really mourning—the **Sunk Cost** anchored in your mind—is those 20 hours of research, the effort of overcoming fear, and the hope you painstakingly built.

You’ll tell yourself: *'I put so much work into understanding this, I can't give up now.'* At this moment, Sunk Cost becomes a double-edged sword: it can be the discipline that helps you reach the harvest, or the chain that keeps you holding a plummeting portfolio because you 'regret' the effort spent.

Before we dive into the numbers, let’s address the ‘why’ behind the variables in this model. In data ~~science~~, your output is only as good as your assumptions.

For this comparison, I’ve set the **Status Quo (Savings) at a 6% annual return**. This isn't an arbitrary choice; it represents the **10-year historical mean** for 12-month VND term deposits in Vietnam from 2014 to 2024, based on State Bank of Vietnam (SBV) data. By using a long-term average rather than today’s specific rate, we create a 'fair-weather' baseline for the safest possible path.

On the other side, I’ve capped the **Index Fund expectation at a conservative 10% CAGR**. While equity markets can deliver higher spikes, 10% serves as a realistic, risk-adjusted benchmark for diversified portfolios like the VN30 over a decade.

We aren't looking for 'best-case scenarios' here. We are looking for the **Risk Premium**—that 4% gap. My goal is to see if that extra 4% is enough to justify the volatility and the 'Sunk Cost' of effort we talked about earlier.

## My own story

I once inherited a project with 3,000 lines of SQL query. My colleague had poured her heart into it. But when the stakeholders requested an update, the logic was so deeply buried that it took the team forever just to understand it, let alone fix it.

Deleting those 3,000 lines felt like a betrayal of her hard work. That 'regret' was a powerful Sunk Cost. But we chose to scrap it and replace it with 100 lines of optimized code. It was a massive upfront time investment to refactor, but the results didn't lie: our processing time dropped from 6 hours to 2 hours. By letting go of the 'effort' of the past, we bought ourselves a future of efficiency.
