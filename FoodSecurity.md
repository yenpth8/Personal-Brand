- Essential information about this data <span title="This section outlines the metric's rationale, its limitations (specifically in interpretation), its inclusions and exclusions, and its intended usage.">ⓘ</span>
    - Daily per capita calorie supply is the amount of calories available to the average person, that does not necessarily equal actual calorie consumption per person.
    - Supply figures do not account for <span title="i.e. food is wasted in retail, restaurant, other household levels"><u>*consumption-level waste*</u></span>, therefore this metric represents food availability for consumption at the retail level rather than total food production.
    - Note: Owing to data scarcity in historical periods, calorie supply and consumption are sometimes used interchangably. <span title="A proxy method where values are assigned using a variable with a closely corresponding meaning">ⓘ</span>
    - The caloric dimension does not provide a complete picture of nutrition, as there are many more components required for a healthy diet. However, it's still treated as one of the most basic criteria of <span title="Food security is defined as the condition in which all people at all times have physical, social, and economic access to sufficient, safe, and nutritious food that meets their dietary needs and preferences for an active and healthy life. It encompasses addressing malnutrition, including chronic hunger and micronutrient deficiencies. &#10Based on: Food Control and Biosecurity (2018)"><u>*food security*</u></span>. Evaluating whether individuals get sufficient calories served as a key input for global malnutrition measurement.
